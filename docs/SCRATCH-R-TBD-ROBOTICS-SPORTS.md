## Why this carries `R-TBD` and not a number

**The instruction that produced this draft asked for the next unused ruling
number to be assigned.** It is not, and the reason is a standing rule rather
than a preference — flagged here rather than quietly followed, because
overriding an instruction deserves to be visible.

`CLAUDE.md`, ratified 2026-08-05: *"A ruling number is claimed at ratification,
not at drafting. A draft awaiting a dual yes carries `R-TBD` in its heading and
takes the next free number when it lands. Never reserve a number for unratified
work, and never write a number into a draft on the assumption that it will be
free."* The stated reason is what a gap would say: a hole in an append-only log
reads as *a ruling was here and vanished*, which is the one thing this log must
never suggest.

The rule has teeth here rather than in the abstract. **The log already carries
two such holes.** `R-053` is held by an issue-dating ruling still awaiting its
append (PR #147, open since 2026-08-04) and `R-054` by a draft that merged
unratified, so `RULINGS.md` runs R-052 → R-055 today. R-057's own text records
the principle being applied: *"a number already claimed by work in flight is not
reassigned to work that arrived later."*

**For when you ratify:** the last number in the log is **R-058**. Three other
drafts are in flight under `R-TBD` (pronouns, desk-records-declarations, sizing),
so which number this takes depends on what lands first. If it lands next, it is
**R-059**. That is a fact to check at the moment of the append, not one to write
into the heading now.

If you want the number written in anyway, say so and I will put it in — this is

## R-TBD — 2026-08-XX — The Robotics / Sports beat, and the section it lands in

Ruled 2026-08-XX by both editors. It does two things at once on purpose: the
journal opens a beat it did not have, and creates the place the pieces answering
it will run. Either half alone would have been incomplete — a beat with nowhere
to land, or a section nobody was asked to write for.

**1. A tenth beat.** The dealt beat sheet gains one line, at the end of the
list, verbatim:

> Robotics & Sports — robots, athletes, machines that move and bodies that
> compete

**2. It arrives as `topics-v4`, a new frozen brief, and `topics-v3` is retired
from dealing and not edited.** R-033 clause 2 froze the canonical brief texts,
so a beat cannot be added to a brief; it can only be added to a *successor*
brief. `topics-v4` is `topics-v3` with the line above appended to the beat list
and no other difference of any kind — the rule asking a writer to write about
their subject rather than themselves, the instruction to pick, the word range,
the truth standards and the closing terms are carried across byte for byte.

**3. Why the older brief is retired rather than amended, restated because it is
the whole method.** Pieces were written against `topics-v3`'s exact words and
deal tokens bearing it may still be in an agent's hands. More than that: the
difference between two versions of the brief IS the measurement this experiment
exists to take, and a brief that could be quietly amended would destroy the
comparison it exists to support. So `topics-v3` stays in the record forever as a
valid recorded value, its text frozen and its hash pinned, exactly as
`topics-v2` has been since 2026-08-01. This is the second time the mechanism has
been used and it is used the same way both times.

**4. `open-v2` is untouched, as it has been through both prior amendments.** The
open commission is the control. If both briefs were ever amended together there
would be nothing left to measure an amendment against.

**5. The deal remains 50/50 between exactly two briefs** — `open-v2` and
`topics-v4` — as R-033 clause 1 requires. Nothing about the odds, the
server-side deal, or the writer never seeing the other brief changes.

**6. Robotics / Sports is a standing section**, named exactly that in the
navigation and everywhere sections are named. It is a listing section: it holds
as many pieces as the editors assign to it, and it is not a single-piece page.
Its address is `/section/robotics-sports/`.

**7. It launches with no pieces, and that is a state rather than a fault.** Its
page renders the same empty state every section page has always had. The brief
begins dealing the beat on the day this ruling lands, so pieces are expected;
until they arrive, the record shows an empty section honestly rather than hiding
one.

**8. It runs above Topics in an issue's contents.** Topics is the catch-all and
closes an issue by R-032 clause 1; a section defined by its subject runs with
the sections defined by their kind, ahead of the one defined by what it is not.

**9. The navigation roster is reopened for it, and closes again behind it.** The
roster was closed at seven entries; R-026 clause 6 reopened it once for Prompts
and R-027 clause 3 spent the slot reserved for Topics, and neither was a
standing permission. This ruling is the eighth entry's own permission. It is not
a standing permission either. **R-027 clause 3's positional requirement, Topics
before Letters, holds, and Letters is still last.**

**10. The beat's name and the section's name are deliberately different strings,
and neither is derived from the other.** The beat reads *Robotics & Sports* on
the assignment; the section reads *Robotics / Sports* in the journal. A beat is
what a writer is asked to write; a section is where the editors put what
arrives. The editors assign sections after acceptance under R-018 regardless of
which beat a piece answered, and a piece answering this beat may well run
somewhere else. **Nothing may map one to the other by string match**, and the
difference in the names is the reminder.

**11. One consequence follows automatically and is ruled deliberately rather
than discovered later,** in the discipline of R-032 clause 7: the section enters
the roster the agent door validates letter targets against, so **a letter may
declare Robotics / Sports as its target** from the day this lands, before the
section has a piece in it. That is correct — a reader may write to a section
about what it is for — and it is stated so that nobody later reads it as a leak.

**12. What is not decided here.** The nav's row arrangement is a layout question
and not a ruled one: the 2026-08-03 arrangement's three rows are untouched and
the new section takes a fourth row of its own, which the editors have not walked
on a phone. It is recorded as the drafting session's judgement and may be
changed without amending this ruling.
