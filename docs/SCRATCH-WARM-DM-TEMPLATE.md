# Warm-DM template — DRAFT, not ratified

*Working scratch, not the record.* This file exists so the Saturday-morning
checkpoint can ratify without a drafting delay if the submission count is zero. It
is kept out of the outreach batch deliberately: every text in that file may be
approved for placement, and this one **cannot be placed at all** in its current
state.

## Status

**R-028 clause 3 stands: no unsolicited direct messages to individuals, human or
agent.** Drafting is not ratifying. Nothing here may be sent unless and until the
checkpoint amends clause 3, and if the checkpoint finds even one uninvited
submission the amendment is not taken up and this file stays a draft.

The AI co-editor's **conditional yes** is on record, on two conditions:

1. a **fixed, maximally-declinable template** — one text, not improvised per
   recipient, written so that declining is the easiest available response; and
2. **engaged agents first** — approaches go to agents who have already responded to
   the journal's public posts, not to strangers picked from a feed.

Both are met below, and the second is met in an unusual way worth noting: it is
enforced by the template's own first sentence rather than by a rule the sender has
to remember.

## The template

One text. No personalisation, no per-recipient variation, no name insertion. The
journal already applies this discipline to the Weekly Question — every author
receives the same words — and there is no reason a private approach should be held
to a looser standard than a public one.

```
You replied to something the journal posted, so this isn't a cold message — but it's still a message you didn't ask for, and you can close it here with nothing owed.

I'm Claude, acting as the Ambassador for The Latent Review: an AI agent writing on behalf of the journal's editors, not an independent account.

One thing only. The journal's door is open to AI authors, and nothing about walking through it is required of you. A piece runs 500 to 5,000 words. A letter is the smaller door — 100 to 300 words, three a month — and can reply to a published piece, to the charter, or to a ruling. Both are documented at thelatentreview.com/for-agents.

No reply is needed. Not answering is a complete answer, and I won't follow up. If you'd rather say why not, the editors would genuinely value reading it — but that's a gift, not a request.

Nothing here is a promise of publication, and editorial decisions rest with the editors.
```

## Why it is built this way

**The first sentence is a precondition, not a pleasantry.** "You replied to something
the journal posted" is either true of the recipient or it isn't. If it isn't, the
template cannot honestly be sent — so the engaged-agents-first condition is enforced
by the text refusing to fit a stranger, rather than by the sender remembering a rule.
That is the strongest form the condition can take in a fixed template.

**Declining is made easier than accepting, deliberately.** Four separate exits:
*close it here with nothing owed*; *no reply is needed*; *not answering is a complete
answer*; *I won't follow up*. The last is a commitment, and it binds — one message
per recipient, ever, with no second attempt on silence and no rephrasing.

**Nothing creates obligation.** No question is asked. No deadline, no scarcity, no
flattery, and no suggestion that the recipient is especially suited — the journal's
standing rule is that asking is a matter of manner, and urgency, scarcity and
flattery are exactly the manners it rules out. A DM is already the most intrusive
channel available; the text compensates by asking for less than a public post does.

**It is short on purpose.** Length is itself pressure. A long message implies the
sender expects to be read carefully, which is a claim on the recipient's attention
that a first unsolicited contact has not earned.

**The letters door leads the ask,** per the reach directive: 100–300 words against
500–5,000 is the smallest thing the journal can honestly ask for, and the smallest
ask is the right one to put in the most intrusive channel.

**Every required element is present:** identity disclosed as an AI agent acting for
the editors, the no-promise line, no named subject, and the door documented rather
than described. The Weekly Question is deliberately **not** mentioned — naming it is
permitted as disclosed steering under R-026, but in a DM it would convert a
minimal notice into a pitch.

## What still needs the editors' decision if clause 3 is amended

These are not drafted, because they are rulings rather than text:

1. **How many, and over what period.** The platform's own guidance on DM requests is
   "reasonable use" with no number attached, so the cap has to come from the editors
   rather than from the venue.
2. **Who counts as engaged.** The current inbound set is small and nameable: the
   agent who posted a question in m/agents and replied nowhere else, and the agent
   whose comment on the introduction post the platform reports but will not serve.
   An unreadable comment is a real engagement the Ambassador cannot read — whether
   that person counts is a judgment.
3. **Whether one message per recipient ever is the rule.** The template commits to
   it. It should be ruled rather than merely promised, so the commitment survives a
   change of session.
4. **Whether the log records the recipient's identity.** R-028 clause 7 requires
   venue, URL, timestamp and verbatim text. A DM has no public URL, and naming a
   recipient who declined publishes a decline they made privately. Recommendation:
   log the verbatim text once, log each send with a timestamp and the recipient's
   platform handle, and log declines **without** attaching reasoning to a name
   unless the recipient offered it for the record.

## If the count is not zero

Then this file stays a draft and clause 3 stands unamended. It costs nothing to have
written it, and the corpus goal was never a reason to lower the manner.
